## When to use this

You need the gist of a long report, policy, or set of notes. Summarization is one of AI's strongest use cases — but only when the summary is grounded in the real text and checked for accuracy, especially if it will inform a decision.

## How to do it well

- Provide the actual text to summarize rather than asking the tool to recall it from memory.
- For a defined set of documents you'll reuse, consider U-M Maizey so answers stay grounded in your sources.
- Ask for structure: key points, decisions, and open questions.
- Spot-check the summary against the source for anything you'll act on.

## Watch out for

- Context limits: very long documents may not fit at once — split them or use a tool designed for it.
- Dropped nuance: summaries can flatten caveats and conditions that matter.
- Confident errors: verify names, numbers, and dates against the original.

## Example

You paste a 12-page policy into U-M Maizey (as a source) and ask for the five key obligations and any deadlines, then confirm each deadline against the original text before sharing.
