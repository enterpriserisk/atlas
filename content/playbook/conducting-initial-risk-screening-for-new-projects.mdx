## When to use this

A new project, system, or vendor relationship is being proposed and you need a quick, structured first-pass screening to decide whether it needs a full risk assessment and at what priority.

## How to do it well

- Give the tool the actual project or vendor description and ask it to structure the intake against your standard screening questions (data involved, user population, integration points, vendor type).
- Ask it to flag which standard risk categories the description touches — data privacy, financial, operational, reputational — so you know what expertise to loop in.
- Use the output to help you decide the next step (full assessment, expedited review, or no further action) — don't let it make that call for you.
- Confirm any risk category it identifies actually applies before routing the request accordingly.

## Watch out for

- A first-pass screening based on limited early information can miss risks that only become clear with more detail — treat it as provisional.
- Don't let a "looks low-risk" screening skip a formal assessment that your policy actually requires based on objective criteria, like data classification or spend threshold.
- Early project descriptions often contain sensitive strategic or vendor-negotiation information — handle accordingly.

## Example

You give U-M GPT a two-paragraph description of a proposed new student-data integration and ask it to flag which risk categories apply and what questions you should ask before scoping the review. You use its category flags to loop in a privacy specialist, but make the actual assessment-priority decision yourself based on your intake policy.
