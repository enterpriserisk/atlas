## When to use this

You need a quick, plain-language orientation to a regulatory area or a recent regulatory change before diving into the official text — a starting point, not the final word.

## How to do it well

- Use the query as a way to identify which official sources to go read, not as the source itself.
- If using a citation-backed search tool like Perplexity, treat it as public-information research only — never for anything involving non-public institutional details — and verify every cited source directly.
- Ask specifically for the effective date and the official body or document name so you can look it up yourself.
- Always retrieve and read the actual current regulatory text before making any compliance determination.

## Watch out for

- Regulatory text changes over time, and AI training data has a cutoff — it may describe an outdated version as current.
- AI can misstate an effective date, an applicability threshold, or an exemption — these details matter enormously in compliance work.
- Never treat an AI summary of a regulation as sufficient basis for a compliance decision — that requires reading the actual current text.

## Example

You ask Perplexity for an overview of a recent change to a data-breach notification requirement. You use its cited sources to locate the actual regulatory text, confirm the effective date directly from the official publication, and base your compliance guidance on that — not the AI summary.
