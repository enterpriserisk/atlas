## When to use this

You've finished a full audit report and need a one-page executive summary highlighting the key findings, risk ratings, and recommendations for readers who won't read the full document.

## How to do it well

- Provide the completed, reviewed audit report as source material; don't ask the tool to audit or assess anything itself.
- Ask for a fixed structure: scope, top findings with ratings, key recommendations, and management response status if applicable.
- Ask it to preserve the exact risk ratings and recommendation language from the full report rather than paraphrasing them loosely.
- Compare the summary against the full report finding-by-finding before it's distributed.

## Watch out for

- A summary can subtly change a finding's severity by rewording it — "significant deficiency" becoming "area for improvement," for example.
- Don't let the tool add a recommendation that isn't in the source report.
- Executive summaries often circulate more widely than the full report — make sure nothing sensitive slipped in that shouldn't be seen by that wider audience.

## Example

You give U-M GPT your 20-page audit report and ask for a one-page executive summary with findings grouped by risk rating. You catch that the draft merged two separate findings into one and split them back out before sending it to leadership.
