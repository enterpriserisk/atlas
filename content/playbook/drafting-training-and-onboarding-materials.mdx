## When to use this

You're building or refreshing training content, a new-hire guide, or a how-to walkthrough for your team, and want a solid first draft to edit rather than starting from scratch.

## How to do it well

- Give the tool your outline or key points and ask for a draft in the format you need (slide notes, a written guide, a short quiz).
- Ask for plain language and a reading level appropriate for someone new to the topic.
- Specify your team's actual current tools, systems, and terminology so the draft doesn't default to generic or outdated examples.
- Have someone currently doing the work review the draft for accuracy before it's used to train anyone.

## Watch out for

- Generic AI training content can describe a "typical" process that doesn't match how your team actually operates.
- Outdated defaults: the tool may reference tool names, steps, or policies that have since changed — verify against current practice.
- Don't include real examples containing confidential data — use realistic but fictional placeholders.

## Example

You outline the five stages of your team's new-vendor-intake process and ask U-M GPT to draft a new-hire training document. Your team lead reviews it, updates one step that changed last quarter, and replaces a placeholder example with your standard fictional scenario.
