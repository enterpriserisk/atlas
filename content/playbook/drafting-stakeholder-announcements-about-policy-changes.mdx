## When to use this

A policy or procedure has changed and you need to communicate it to affected staff — what changed, why, and what they need to do differently.

## How to do it well

- Give the tool your own accurate summary of the change (see also: comparing policy document versions) as source material, not the policy itself to interpret alone.
- Ask for a structure that separates what changed, why, and any action required, with an effective date clearly stated.
- Specify tone and audience — a broad staff announcement reads differently than a message to a small working group.
- Have the policy owner confirm the announcement accurately reflects the change before it's sent broadly.

## Watch out for

- An announcement that's clearer than the policy itself can also be subtly wrong — verify it says exactly what changed, no more and no less.
- Don't let the tool invent a rationale for the change that wasn't actually given.
- Broad announcements are hard to unsend — this is exactly the kind of external/broad-facing content that always needs review.

## Example

You summarize a change to your data-retention policy and ask U-M GPT to draft a staff-wide announcement with an effective date and a short "what you need to do" section. The policy owner confirms the description is accurate and adjusts the effective date before it's sent.
