## When to use this

Any time AI output includes factual claims, statistics, quotations, or references that you intend to use or share. This is the single most important habit for responsible AI use.

## How to do it well

- Trace each fact to an authoritative primary source before relying on it.
- Confirm that every citation actually exists and says what's claimed — AI citations are frequently invented.
- Be most skeptical of precise numbers, dates, names, and quotes.
- Prefer grounding the tool in sources you provide over asking from memory.

## Watch out for

- Fabricated references: plausible-looking titles, authors, or section numbers that don't exist.
- Outdated information: models may not know recent changes.
- "It sounds right" bias: fluency is not accuracy.

## Example

An AI summary cites "State of Michigan Directive 2024-11." Before including it, you search for that directive, find it doesn't exist, and remove the claim — a classic hallucinated citation caught by verification.
