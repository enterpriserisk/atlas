## When to use this

You have a table of metrics you've already produced — incident counts, audit findings closed, training completion rates, quarter-over-quarter trends — and need a narrative paragraph or two for a report or briefing.

## How to do it well

- Give the tool the actual numbers you calculated; ask only for prose describing them, not to compute or estimate anything.
- Ask for a plain-language explanation of what changed and by how much, in a tone appropriate for the audience (see also: translating risk findings for leadership).
- Ask the tool to flag any number in your table that looks internally inconsistent (like a percentage that doesn't add up) as a prompt to double-check — not as a correction.

## Watch out for

- Never ask the tool to do the arithmetic, extrapolate a trend, or fill in a missing data point — it can produce a plausible-sounding but wrong number.
- Verify every figure in the generated narrative matches your source table exactly before it goes into a report.
- Watch for the tool inserting comparative language ("significantly," "dramatically") that overstates a modest change.

## Example

You give U-M GPT a table showing incident counts fell from 42 to 31 quarter over quarter and ask for two sentences summarizing the trend for a leadership dashboard. You check that both numbers and the percentage change in the output match your table before including it.
