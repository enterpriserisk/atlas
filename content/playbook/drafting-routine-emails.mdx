## When to use this

Routine, low-stakes internal messages — meeting recaps, scheduling notes, status updates — that don't contain confidential or personal data. AI is a good fit here because the risk of error is low and a human still reviews before sending.

## How to do it well

- Give the tool the key points as bullets and ask for a short, plain-language draft.
- Specify the audience and tone (e.g., "brief and friendly, for my team").
- Keep confidential specifics out of the prompt — refer to them generally if needed.
- Read and edit the draft so it sounds like you and is accurate.

## Watch out for

- Invented details: AI may add specifics you didn't provide. Remove anything untrue.
- Tone drift: routine emails can come out overly formal or padded — trim them.
- Never paste personal or regulated data into a tool not approved for it.

## Example

You jot three bullets summarizing a meeting, ask U-M GPT for a two-paragraph recap for your team, then edit the draft to fix one date and cut a filler sentence before sending.
