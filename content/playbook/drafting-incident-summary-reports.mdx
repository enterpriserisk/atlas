## When to use this

Shortly after a risk or security incident, when you need to turn raw notes, timelines, or witness accounts into a structured initial summary for internal review.

## How to do it well

- Provide your own factual notes as the source material; don't ask the tool to fill gaps with assumptions about what "probably" happened.
- Ask for a standard structure — what happened, when, who was involved, immediate actions taken, and open questions — without asking it to assign fault or root cause.
- Explicitly flag any detail you're not certain about so it's marked as unconfirmed in the draft, not stated as fact.
- Route the draft through your normal incident-review process before it's finalized or shared beyond the immediate team.

## Watch out for

- Confident-sounding language can make an uncertain detail read as an established fact — watch for this especially around timelines and causes.
- Incident details are frequently sensitive or regulated — confirm the tool and your workflow are approved for the data involved before you start.
- Don't let a first-draft summary substitute for your organization's formal incident-response and root-cause process.

## Example

You give U-M GPT your timestamped notes from the first hour of an incident and ask for a structured summary with a clearly marked "unconfirmed details" section. You correct one timestamp and remove a speculative cause the tool inferred before sharing the draft with your team lead.
