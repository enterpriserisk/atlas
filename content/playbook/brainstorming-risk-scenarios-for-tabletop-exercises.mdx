## When to use this

You're planning a tabletop exercise or scenario-planning session and want a broad first pass at plausible risk scenarios to react to, refine, or combine — before your team narrows in on what's realistic for your context.

## How to do it well

- Ask for a range of scenarios across categories (operational, cyber, reputational, financial, compliance) rather than one at a time, so you get breadth to react to.
- Ask the tool to vary severity and likelihood so you get both "likely and manageable" and "unlikely but severe" scenarios to choose from.
- Use the output as a prompt for group discussion — the value is in what your team adds and challenges, not the AI draft itself.
- Have a subject-matter expert sanity-check any scenario before it's used in a real exercise, to confirm it's actually plausible for your institution.

## Watch out for

- Generic scenarios: AI may generate plausible-sounding but generic scenarios that don't reflect your institution's actual risk profile.
- Don't treat AI-generated likelihood or severity ratings as a real risk assessment — they're a discussion starting point only.
- Avoid including any real, specific vulnerability details in the prompt if the exercise will be shared broadly.

## Example

You ask U-M GPT for eight varied risk scenarios spanning cyber, operational, and reputational categories for a departmental tabletop exercise. Your planning team selects three, discards two as unrealistic for your context, and substantially rewrites the rest before using them in the exercise.
