## When to use this

After a risk committee meeting or similar working session, when you have raw notes (or a transcript) and need a clean, structured first draft of the official minutes.

## How to do it well

- Provide your actual notes or transcript as the source; ask for a structured draft (attendees, decisions, action items with owners and dates).
- Ask the tool to clearly separate what was decided from what was merely discussed.
- If you use a transcription tool for the meeting itself, get consent from all attendees first and never use it for meetings touching confidential or regulated topics — see the Tool Directory entry for transcription tools.
- Review the draft against your own notes line by line before it's distributed as the official record.

## Watch out for

- AI may attribute a comment or decision to the wrong person, or invent an action item that wasn't actually agreed to.
- Minutes are a record people rely on later — an error here compounds if it isn't caught before distribution.
- Never paste content from a meeting that touched confidential personnel, legal, or regulated matters into a general-purpose tool.

## Example

You dictate rough notes right after a risk committee meeting and ask U-M GPT to draft minutes with a decisions/action-items structure. You catch that it assigned an action item to the wrong committee member and correct it before circulating the draft for approval.
