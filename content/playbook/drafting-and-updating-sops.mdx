## When to use this

You're writing a new standard operating procedure or updating an existing one, and want a consistent structure and clear language without starting from a blank page each time.

## How to do it well

- Provide the actual steps as you understand them (bullets are fine); ask the tool to structure and clarify, not invent missing steps.
- Ask for a consistent format across your team's SOPs (purpose, scope, steps, roles, related documents) so your library stays uniform.
- Ask it to flag any step that seems to assume knowledge or context a new reader wouldn't have.
- Have the person who actually performs the process review the draft for accuracy before it's published.

## Watch out for

- AI may smooth over an ambiguous or missing step with a plausible-sounding one that isn't actually correct.
- Don't let the tool add compliance or regulatory claims ("this satisfies X requirement") — that determination needs a human with the relevant expertise.
- Keep version history and review dates current per your document-control process; AI won't track this for you.

## Example

You give U-M GPT a rough bullet list of the steps for onboarding a new vendor and ask for a formatted SOP matching your team's standard template. The person who normally runs this process reviews it and adds a missing approval step before it's published.
