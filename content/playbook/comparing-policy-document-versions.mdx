## When to use this

A policy, standard, or procedure has been revised and you need to understand what actually changed before communicating it or updating dependent guidance.

## How to do it well

- Provide both versions of the full text directly — don't rely on the tool's memory of either document.
- Ask for a structured list of substantive changes (added/removed requirements, changed deadlines or thresholds) rather than a general "what's different" summary.
- Cross-check the output against your organization's official redline or version-control diff, if one exists — that's the authoritative source.
- Use the summary to guide your own read-through, not as a replacement for it.

## Watch out for

- AI can miss subtle wording changes that shift meaning (e.g., "should" becoming "must") without flagging them as substantive.
- It can also invent a "change" that isn't really there — verify every item against the actual text.
- If using a tool like NotebookLM, confirm current University approval status before uploading policy documents — see the Tool Directory.

## Example

You upload the 2025 and 2026 versions of a data-handling standard and ask for every changed requirement. The tool flags five items; you confirm all five against the redline and catch one additional wording change it missed before updating your team's guidance.
